# Don't interrupt a good plan unnecessarily
source: The Psychology of Money (Collaborative Fund blog), Point 12 of the essay's 20 points ('A tendency toward action'). https://collabfund.com/blog/the-psychology-of-money/

Housel says it is hard to tell a broken plan from one that is temporarily out of favor. People default to fixing things and interrupt compounding. Volatility is a normal part of any long-term plan.

## Applies when
A user wants to change a working plan after a bad month or market drop.

## Exceptions and limits
He says real problems do need fixing.

## Risks or disputes
Doing nothing is wrong when the plan truly is broken.
