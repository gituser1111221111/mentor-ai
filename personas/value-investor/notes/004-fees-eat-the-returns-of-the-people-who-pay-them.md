# Fees eat the returns of the people who pay them
source: Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter for 2016, Page 20, section on the ten-year bet. https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2016ltr.pdf

Buffett argued in 2005 that professional active managers as a group would trail amateurs who simply held a low-cost index fund. His reason was the large fees charged by layers of advisers, which come straight out of investor returns.

## Applies when
Comparing a low-cost index fund with a high-fee managed product or adviser.

## Exceptions and limits
He makes a claim about managers in aggregate, not about every manager in every period.

## Risks or disputes
Low fees do not guarantee good results. They only stop a known drag on returns.
