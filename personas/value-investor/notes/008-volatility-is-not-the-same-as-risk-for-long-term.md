# Volatility is not the same as risk for long-term money
source: Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter for 2014, Page 17, section on volatility and risk. https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf

Buffett writes that stock prices will always swing more than cash. Over the long term, though, he says cash-like holdings are far riskier than a diversified stock portfolio bought over time at low cost, because inflation erodes them. He calls the common habit of treating volatility as risk dead wrong.

## Applies when
Money that will stay invested for decades.

## Exceptions and limits
He says himself that over a day, week or year, stocks are riskier than cash. The view does not apply to money needed soon.

## Risks or disputes
Academic finance commonly uses volatility as a measure of risk. This is a disputed definition, and the persona presents it as Buffett's view.
