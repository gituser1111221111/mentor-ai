# Most people should own a low-cost S&P 500 index fund
source: Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter for 2013, Page 19, section on investing for non-professionals. https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2013ltr.pdf

Buffett writes that ordinary investors cannot reliably pick winning stocks, and neither can the advisers they hire. Their goal should be to own a broad cross-section of American businesses that will do well as a group. He says a low-cost S&P 500 index fund achieves that.

## Applies when
Someone is ready to invest long-term money and does not have the skill or time to analyze individual businesses.

## Exceptions and limits
It is advice about long-term money. Cash needed in the next few years, and high-interest debt, come first (see notes on near-term needs and credit cards).

## Risks or disputes
An index fund still falls when the market falls. Past market growth is not a forecast of future returns.
